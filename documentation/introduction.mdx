## Don’t ask what to choose. Explore what each choice changes.

**Fork** turns a difficult decision into **a set of paths you can explore**. You describe what you’re deciding in a sentence or two. Fork maps it into **2–4 materially different paths** and shows what each one changes, its tradeoffs and what it assumes. You can compare the paths side by side. Then **you** choose, and Fork keeps a note of why in a private decision journal.

Fork never tells you what to do. It never ranks the options and never claims to predict the future.

Fork is one codebase that runs on **iOS, Android and the web**. It is built with Expo and React Native. A Supabase Edge Function acts as the AI proxy (Google Gemini or Anthropic Claude), and **RevenueCat** runs the **Fork Pro** subscription.

## The problem Fork solves

Hard decisions stall for three reasons:

<Steps>
  <Step title="The options stay fuzzy">
    “Buy now or wait?” hides a third option (fix what you have) and a dozen unspoken assumptions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The tradeoffs are invisible">
    You weigh cost against peace of mind in your head, and one worry drowns out everything else.
  </Step>
  <Step title="AI chatbots answer instead of mapping">
    Chatbots are built to answer. They pick for you, sound more certain than they should, and leave you with a wall of text instead of a map.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Fork’s answer is **interactive decision scenarios**:

| | |
|---|---|
| **A fork, not an answer** | Your decision is drawn as an animated branching tree. Each branch (A–D) is a path you can tap into. |
| **Paths are structured, not prose** | Each path is a set of short cards: what changes immediately, upside, tradeoffs, risks, assumptions, uncertainty, what would change it, and questions worth answering. |
| **Compare on what matters to you** | You pick the dimensions you care about (cost, time, flexibility, risk…). The rows where the paths differ most rise to the top. There are no scores and no winner. |
| **You decide, and remember why** | You pick the path you’re leaning toward (or “still deciding”), write a note to your future self and keep it in your journal. |
